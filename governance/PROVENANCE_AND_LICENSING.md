## Visible provenance rather than an invisible watermark
- Keep author, project URL, source version and license notices near reusable materials.
- Cite CITATION.cff. Do not cite development work as peer-reviewed results.
- Preserve the original submitted PDF bytes. Any later branded/licensed edition must have a different filename and explicit change/date note.
- Use release manifests and SHA256 for exact-file integrity. A hash is not a detector of translated, excerpted or paraphrased reuse.
- Statistical generative watermarks require an actual embedding/detection scheme; arbitrary phrasing or zero-width characters do not provide reliable provenance.
- Similarity/fingerprint matches can generate leads for manual checking; they do not establish a legal violation or recover cognitive authorship.

With an open license, compliant copying is authorized reuse, not theft. Tracking should target lost attribution or license noncompliance, not punish permitted reuse. Attribution duties apply to licensed material in their actual legal scope; a project idea alone is not made exclusive by a watermark.
