# HALA — PISA AI-Mediated Assessment Research Proposal
**Working draft v0.2 · 8 October 2026 · Not submitted**

**Proposer:** Pyragogy, an independent research initiative. Lead: Fabrizio Terzi.  
**Contact:** To be supplied in submission.  
**Purpose:** Bounded research collaboration; subject to OECD confirmation of eligibility, financing, IP and ethics arrangements.

## 1. Proposed solution and PISA relevance

**HALA — Human–AI Learning Assessment** investigates a fundamental measurement problem: when learners use conversational AI, how can assessment distinguish proficiency shown *through* that collaboration from performance attributable mainly to the AI?

We propose an AI-mediated assessment demonstrator for students in approximately the PISA age range. In three newly designed interactive assessment tasks on causal reasoning, evidence evaluation and source reliability, learners would investigate realistic claims, question AI outputs, identify competing explanations, revise an artefact and justify their final judgment. The **three tasks are proposed deliverables, not existing validated items**.

Two prespecified AI roles would permit comparison of interaction formats: an **explanatory assistant** offering bounded clarifications, and a **critical collaborator** requesting counterexamples, verification and justified revision. Development would hold domain, time and tool access as comparable as feasible. Both roles would generate time-ordered process data, candidate rubric observations and a student-produced final artefact.

The central innovation is a **dual evidence design**. First, researchers would examine whether blinded scoring of student–AI interaction trajectories yields stable, interpretable proficiency signals (e.g., evaluating evidence, checking assumptions, explaining revisions), and whether those signals can be compared across prompt/model conditions. Second, *independent response anchors* and novel transfer questions would support investigation of what the conversational score actually measures, mitigating possible confusion between AI-provided answers and student competence. A delayed follow-up could be explored only in a separately authorised study.

The current private HALA repository includes synthetic-only journey prototypes, conditional scripted dialogue, provenance-oriented tests and a two-process assessment fixture. **It has no live AI tutor, enrolled students, calibrated scoring, psychometric validation, or secure field deployment.**

**Model plan requiring joint confirmation:** a version-pinned, contractually permissible hosted language model for initial text-only development, tested against a self-hosted/open-weight alternative to reduce dependence on a single provider. Before delivery, we would specify precise model IDs and revisions, inference settings, deployment jurisdiction, latency, token use, privacy and fallbacks. **No live model is selected, licensed or deployed yet**; model selection is a milestone rather than an established asset.

**Initial bounded outputs:** three prototype task specifications, role/prompt version registry, a process-evidence coding manual and provisional scoring rubric, reproducible synthetic trace package, deployment/data protection design, and a jointly agreed feasibility/validation plan. Claims of score validity, cross-country comparability or improved student learning would be withheld pending actual evidence.

## 2. Collaboration with OECD

Pyragogy could contribute assessment concepts, working prototype engineering, methodological documentation, trace reproducibility and adversarial software testing. We propose **biweekly design reviews** with the PISA RDI team during a bounded co-design phase, followed by an agreed technical and psychometric review gate.

We would request OECD guidance on: target competency constructs and PISA delivery requirements; multilingual adaptation and equivalence; student safeguarding and data-processing expectations; suitable scoring/equating strategies; and availability of authorised pilot partners, psychometric specialists and participating sites. **None of these external inputs or partnerships is secured today.** Any pilot using minors would occur only through an approved partner and governance process, not as an automatic deliverable of the initial prototype.

## 3. Intellectual property

Pyragogy proposes to distinguish **background IP** (existing independently developed methods, documentation and source code) from **foreground IP** co-developed for the PISA research collaboration. A bounded research/evaluation licence to the OECD may be negotiated, with scope, reuse, publication, confidentiality and ownership agreed in writing. All model-provider, dataset, library and third-party rights would be inventoried before release. No current OECD licence, endorsement or partnership is implied.

## 4. Privacy, security and feasibility

The prototype is **synthetic only**, local and not authorised for participant data. For any future student-facing pilot, a formal data-flow assessment, minimisation, pseudonymisation, appropriate consent/legal basis, restricted retention/deletion, controlled access, logging and incident procedures would be required. Assessment items and rubrics would be separated from tutor/model context under independently tested process and account boundaries. The current system does **not yet meet** those deployment requirements.

**No collected student data would be permitted for model-provider training** under a future agreement; this must be verified through technical configuration and enforceable provider terms (or by an approved self-hosted implementation), not presumed. Cross-border processing, child protections, model updates, model leakage and source contamination would be assessed before a field trial.

Full-scale feasibility would be evaluated through per-session inference and infrastructure costs, fixed/alternative model options, throughput, languages, latency and failure modes. The call does not remunerate the chosen partner. Pyragogy therefore proposes a bounded co-design commitment first; broader deployment, recruitment and validation would be **conditional upon partners, external financing and approvals**.

**Request:** consideration as a research/development collaborator for a bounded proof-of-concept in valid AI-mediated student assessment, subject to written clarification of independent-researcher eligibility and call conditions.
