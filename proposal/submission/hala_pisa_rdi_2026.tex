\section{Proposed solution and PISA relevance}

\noindent HALA proposes a bounded OECD co-design programme to establish when
evidence from student--AI dialogue can validly support PISA proficiency
inferences.  Existing assessment frameworks---PISA 2015 Collaborative Problem
Solving (controlled computer agents), dynamic assessment (scaffolded performance
as evidence), PISA 2025 Learning in the Digital World (process and response
evidence)---show that interaction data can support proficiency claims under
specific design conditions.\footnote{OECD (2026), \emph{PISA 2025 Assessment and
Analytical Framework}, \url{https://doi.org/10.1787/86c36975-en}; OECD,
\emph{Innovating Assessments to Measure and Support Complex Skills}, ch.\ on
Evidence-Centred Design.} HALA tests a narrower question that these frameworks
leave open: can \emph{generated, adaptive AI dialogue}---where the assistant
proposes explanations, counterexamples and requests---yield evidence that
supports valid inference about \emph{learner proficiency}, and under what
design conditions?

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Two candidate constructs.} The programme evaluates
both \textbf{unaided proficiency} (what a learner can do when the AI provides
only generic interaction management) and \textbf{AI-supported problem-solving
proficiency} (how effectively a learner uses AI-generated information without
being passively led).  Which observations warrant which inference is the object
of study, jointly specified with the OECD before any data collection.  Agent
variance is not assumed to be a contaminant: scaffold responsiveness may itself
be construct-relevant.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Experimental skeleton.} Three synthetic tasks
(science source appraisal, correlational reasoning, controlled comparison) are
crossed with three AI agent policies (explanatory assistant, critical
collaborator, generic management) and two task languages (English, one European
partner language).  Student turns are nested within tasks and sessions; agent
turns are separately versioned and logged.  Independent unaided probes provide
external validity anchors.  Rater calibration and blinding are used where
feasible.  Decision classes (reliability thresholds, agent-task effect sizes,
convergence with probes, differential code functioning across languages) are
preregistered before pilot data are analysed; final thresholds, sample sizes
and rater counts are co-designed with the OECD, not fabricated here.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Observation and provenance.} Each student turn is
annotated with a behaviour code, the preceding AI intervention, and the
flag \texttt{learner\_origin\ =\ indeterminate}---the system does not identify
cognitive authorship.  A separate field \texttt{code\_status} distinguishes
\texttt{sufficient\_evidence} (code fires with adequate behavioural basis)
from \texttt{insufficient\_evidence} (short assent, off-task, or response
too minimal to evaluate).  Draft codes include \textbf{EVIDENCE\_REQUEST}
(student requests grounds),
\textbf{COMPETING\_CAUSE} (proposes rival explanation),
\textbf{DISCRIMINATING\_TEST} (designs a comparison),
\textbf{SOURCE\_APPRAISAL} (evaluates credibility),
\textbf{JUSTIFIED\_REVISION} (revises with evidence), and
\textbf{CALIBRATED\_UNCERTAINTY} (proportional confidence).
These are falsification instruments, not a scoring system.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Worked example.}  AI:~``Could the correlation reflect
a third factor?''  Student:~``Maybe kids who use screens at night also sleep
less for other reasons.''  Rater tags \texttt{COMPETING\_CAUSE};
\texttt{learner\_origin\ =\ indeterminate}.  The student's phrase \emph{at
night} follows the AI's \emph{evening use}---a lexical uptake signal, not
evidence of copying or independent authorship.  The code fires because the
student proposes a rival explanation rather than merely assenting; a response
``Yeah, maybe'' receives \texttt{code\_status\ =\ insufficient\_evidence} even
if raters agree on the surface behaviour.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Measurement development.} Codes are developed through
a predeclared five-step sequence: (1) define minimum observation opportunities
per code; (2) annotate student action and preceding AI intervention separately;
(3) estimate inter-rater agreement and code-distribution shifts under different
agents and prompt versions; (4) compare conversational indicators against
independent unaided probes and examine language comparability; (5) retain only
indicators that survive these tests---those that track prompting are discarded.
Negative results are reported explicitly.

\section{Collaboration with OECD}

\noindent Three bounded work packages are proposed:

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Package 1 --- Construct and estimand agreement.}
OECD specifies target competency constructs, delivery requirements, multilingual
equivalence standards, and safeguarding expectations.  HALA produces a
measurement-design document distinguishing unaided and AI-supported constructs
and defining the candidate \texttt{learner\_origin} and fallback rules.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Package 2 --- Tasks, prompts, and observation
instrument.} HALA delivers three task specifications with version-controlled
prompt registries, a behavioural coding manual with worked examples, a
reproducible synthetic trace pipeline, and an adversarial test suite for
prompt-contamination detection---all developed through iterative co-design
with OECD experts.  OECD reviews task constructs, prompt policies for
cross-language consistency, and data-security requirements.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Package 3 --- Instrument review and pilot-readiness
gate.} HALA produces a coding-manual reliability report based on calibration
data, a data-protection design (DPIA-ready framework, pseudonymisation,
lawful-basis analysis, controller/processor allocation, contractual training
exclusion, retention/deletion schedules and transfer controls), and a written
assessment of conditions under which a student-facing pilot becomes feasible.
OECD provides authorised pilot access, psychometric expertise, and ethics
approval processes.  \textbf{No pilot deployment can proceed without agreed
resources, partners, ethics approvals, and financing.  The initial co-design
phase (Packages~1--2) can proceed without institutional funding.}

\section{Intellectual property and model choice}

\noindent\textbf{IP:} Source code and documentation developed before
collaboration (background) remain private without an open-source licence;
co-developed designs and specifications (foreground) are offered to the OECD
under a bounded, non-exclusive research licence to be negotiated in writing
before joint work begins.  Third-party model rights and provider terms are
not licensed by the proposer.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Models:} No model or provider is selected.  HALA
prioritises EU-hosted and independently deployable candidates in a
provider-independent strategy.  Mistral Large~4 (released 6 October 2026,
public preview via Mistral Studio at time of writing; self-deployment terms to
be confirmed) is a candidate, not a selection.  Other candidates include OpenAI
GPT-4o snapshot \texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06} and at least one independently
deployable open-weight model; none is preselected.  Model versions and
interaction policies are fixed within each evaluative phase; updates require a
documented new phase.  EU data residency and contractual prohibition of
training on student data are verified, not assumed from provider nationality.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Full-scale cost dimensions:} model inference tokens
(including dialogue history, retries and auxiliary calls), human coding and
rater moderation, translation and adaptation, psychometric analysis, platform
operations and monitoring, and security review.  No per-session price or
zero-cost self-hosting is presumed.  A detailed cost formula for reproducible
inference budgets is maintained in the project assumptions document; published
tariffs and actual workloads are tested before deployment choice is made.

\section{Privacy, security, and feasibility}

\noindent\textbf{Current state:} synthetic prototype only---private VPS, no
real participant data, no personal information stored.  Architecture:
two-process separation with HMAC-authenticated IPC and internal security review;
no independent certification is claimed.  OS-level user isolation is not
deployed.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Preconditions for any student-facing pilot:}
completed DPIA, pseudonymisation, lawful basis under GDPR/DPA with
controller/processor allocation (not automatic parental-consent assumption),
contractual and technical prohibition of model-provider training on student
data, retention/deletion schedules, subprocessor and international-transfer
controls, incident-response procedures, child-safeguarding mechanisms (prompt
monitoring, escalation, access logging, human-review boundaries), and
appropriate ethics approvals and institutional partnerships---none of which
exist or are claimed today.  The prototype is a research instrument for a
prospective programme, not an operational assessment platform.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Readiness.}  This proposal describes a planned research
programme, not an operational system.  No validated scores, student data,
ethics approval, institutional partnership, or deployed AI tutor exist.  The
research question---whether evidence from AI-mediated dialogue can validly
support proficiency inferences---is the object of study, not a capability
claim.  Controlled repository access can be offered to OECD evaluators on
request.
