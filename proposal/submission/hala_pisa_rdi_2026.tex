\section{Proposed solution and PISA relevance}

\noindent\textbf{The assessment challenge.} Interactive assessment already recognises
that a final answer need not tell the whole story. Process data, use of
feedback, strategic choices and revisions can provide additional evidence
of how learners approach complex tasks. OECD's PISA 2025
\emph{Learning in the Digital World} assessment, for instance,
combines response and process evidence in interactive environments.

Conversational AI introduces an additional interpretive challenge.
Unlike a static resource, an AI assistant may propose an explanation,
suggest a counterexample or ask a question that changes the direction
of the student's next response. A student who identifies a competing
cause after the AI asks whether a third factor could explain a correlation
has produced an observable action; it is not yet evidence that the student
could have generated that reasoning independently. Conversely, a
learner may understand an issue but not express it in a form that an
interaction code recognises. An apparent increase in reasoning
behaviours might therefore reflect the assistant's prompting policy,
the learner's competence, or their combination.

\noindent\textbf{Existing foundations and HALA's specific question.}
Evidence-Centred Design, argument-based validation and analysis of
assessment process data provide substantial theoretical and operational
foundations. HALA does not claim these frameworks are absent or
ineffective. Instead, it investigates a narrower empirical question:
\emph{under what conditions can process evidence from AI-mediated
dialogue support defensible claims about learner proficiency?}
This requires distinguishing what the learner visibly does, what the
AI has supplied, and what can legitimately be inferred from their
interaction. We do not presume that independent authorship can be
recovered from a dialogue transcript, nor that every observation
should become a proficiency score.

\noindent\textbf{Core research programme.} HALA proposes to test \emph{when}
evidence from student--AI dialogue can be attributed to student proficiency
rather than to the interaction policy or the AI's scaffolding. Three questions guide the investigation:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
  \item Which student reasoning behaviours are observable and codifiable with
    sufficient reliability across raters and tasks?
  \item How much do these behavioural indicators change when the AI agent,
    its prompting style, and the task structure vary?
  \item Which indicators survive comparison with independent unaided probes
    and tests of cross-linguistic comparability?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Interpretation and falsification.} Human raters may
agree on an observable behaviour without that behaviour establishing
independent competence. HALA will retain candidate indicators only
when evidence supports reliability, adequate independence from agent
prompting, and appropriate convergence with unaided probes and
cross-linguistic checks. Inconclusive or negative results will be
reported explicitly rather than repackaged as validated scores.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Illustrative scenario} (hypothetical synthetic stimuli, for
illustration only---no real empirical studies are cited). A student evaluates
two \emph{synthetic} source cards about screen use and sleep: a
correlational claim and an apparently conflicting experimental result. The AI agent proposes an interpretation
(\textquoteleft Screen time may not affect sleep directly; the correlation could
reflect other factors like evening use\textquoteright). The student can accept,
challenge, request additional evidence, or formulate an alternative explanation.
Two AI roles---explanatory assistant (bounded Socratic prompts) and critical
collaborator (counterexample and verification requests)---enable systematic
comparison across interaction conditions.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Worked observable (for the rater manual):} AI turn:\enspace
\textquoteleft Could the correlation reflect a third factor?\textquoteright\enspace Student turn:\enspace
\textquoteleft Maybe kids who use screens at night also sleep less for other reasons\textquoteright.
Rater tags \texttt{COMPETING\_CAUSE}: the student proposes a rival explanation
without asserting it as fact. Failure case: student replies \textquoteleft Yeah,
maybe\textquoteright after a similar AI prompt; the rater records
\texttt{undetermined}---no code fires. Because the AI has already suggested the third-factor direction,
this tag is not an ability score or an authorship finding:
\texttt{learner\_origin\ =\ indeterminate}. Short unaided probes would serve as
external validity anchors, with order and reactivity effects explicitly tested.\par

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Measurement development sequence.} Observational codes will
not be presented as a validated instrument at the start of the collaboration.
They will be developed through a predeclared sequence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
  \item Define the observation opportunities each task must provide (minimum
    evidence for each code to be scorable);
  \item Annotate the student action and the preceding AI intervention
    separately, preserving turn-level provenance;
  \item Evaluate inter-rater agreement and test whether code distributions
    shift when the AI agent, prompt version, or task changes;
  \item Compare conversational indicators against independent unaided probes
    and examine language comparability;
  \item Retain only indicators that survive these tests; discard those that
    primarily track prompting.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Candidate codes (draft) include: \textbf{EVIDENCE\_REQUEST}
(student requests grounds for a claim), \textbf{COMPETING\_CAUSE} (proposes
rival explanation), \textbf{DISCRIMINATING\_TEST} (designs comparison to
distinguish hypotheses), \textbf{SOURCE\_APPRAISAL} (evaluates credibility),
\textbf{JUSTIFIED\_REVISION} (revises with evidence link), and
\textbf{CALIBRATED\_UNCERTAINTY} (proportional confidence). Each is
recorded per turn with \texttt{learner\_origin\ =\ indeterminate}---the
system does not identify the cognitive author. A heuristic annotation
pipeline exists (adversarial testing against naive surface scoring) but
produces no proficiency estimate; it is a falsification instrument, not
a scoring system.

\section{Collaboration with OECD}

\noindent\textbf{Proposer delivers:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
  \item Three task specifications with version-controlled prompt registries
    and a process-coding manual, developed through iterative co-design;
  \item A reproducible synthetic interaction trace pipeline and adversarial
    test suite for detecting prompt-contamination;
  \item A deployment and data-protection design (DPIA-ready framework)
    and a written report on the conditions under which a pilot would
    become feasible.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{OECD guidance required:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
  \item Target competency constructs and PISA delivery requirements;
  \item Multilingual adaptation and equivalence standards;
  \item Student safeguarding and data-processing expectations;
  \item Access to authorised pilot partners and psychometric specialists.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{None of these external inputs or partnerships is secured
today.} We propose a bounded, jointly scoped co-design phase;
subsequent pilot deployment depends on agreed resources, ethics approvals,
and partners. The call provides no partner remuneration.

\section{Privacy, security, and feasibility}

\noindent\textbf{Current state:} the HALA prototype is synthetic only---it
runs on a private VPS, generates no real participant data, and stores no
personal information. Architecture: two-process separation (gateway and
worker) with HMAC-authenticated IPC, audited but uncertified. OS-level
user isolation is not deployed.

\noindent\textbf{Required for any student-facing pilot:} data protection
impact assessment, pseudonymisation, appropriate legal basis under
GDPR/DPA (not automatic parental consent under Art.~8 alone---roles of
controller and processor must be established with the partner institution),
and contractual prohibition on model-provider training from assessment data.

\noindent\textbf{Model choice and scale.} No model or provider is selected.
\textbf{European-first model strategy.} HALA will prioritise EU-hosted and
independently deployable AI models, including Mistral Large 4 (announced 6
October 2026; available in public preview via Mistral Studio) as a candidate
for post-selection evaluation, without committing to a self-hosted trial at
this stage. Final model selection will be conducted jointly with the OECD
against predefined criteria for measurement quality, data protection,
multilingual comparability, reproducibility and deployment costs. Other
candidates remain under consideration (e.g.\ OpenAI GPT-4o snapshot
\texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}, an Anthropic Claude API model subject to
availability, and self-hosted open-weight alternatives); none is preselected
or excluded. Model versions and interaction policies will be fixed before
each evaluative phase; updates require a documented new phase, not an
unrecorded substitution. EU data residency and exclusion of student data
from model training will be verified contractually and technically, not
assumed from provider nationality. Student data must not be used for model
training.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Cost and provider-dependence gate.} For each candidate, the
reproducible inference budget is
\[C={\textstyle\frac{N_{\rm in}P_{\rm in}+N_{\rm out}P_{\rm out}}{10^6}}+C_{\rm compute}+C_{\rm other}\],
where token volumes include full dialogue history, retries and auxiliary
calls. Published token tariffs, actual workloads, latency, hosting and
privacy guarantees will be tested before choosing a deployment. No
unsupported per-session price or zero-cost self-hosting is assumed.
