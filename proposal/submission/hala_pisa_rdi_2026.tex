\section{Proposed solution and PISA relevance}

\noindent\textbf{The measurement problem.} When a student solves a task through
dialogue with an AI, the observed response reflects both the student's competence
and the AI's scaffolding. These two contributions are not separable from the
final answer alone. A superficially correct response may mask the absence of
independent reasoning; a tentative or fragmented answer may reflect genuine
proficiency that the interaction format did not elicit. PISA's transition toward
AI-mediated assessment makes this ambiguity a validity threat.

\textbf{Dual-evidence design.} HALA addresses this through two parallel
measurement channels: (1) blinded scoring of the student--AI interaction
trajectory using behaviourally anchored observational codes, and (2)
independent response anchors---short unaided probes interspersed during the
task---that estimate what the student can produce without AI support. The
comparison between the interaction-based score and the independent-probe
score provides an empirical estimate of the AI's contribution, making the
proficiency signal interpretable rather than assumed.

\noindent\textbf{Proposed task family (specifications, not field-tested).}
Three interactive tasks are proposed for development:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
  \item \textbf{Causal claim.} Students evaluate a proposed cause--effect
    relationship (e.g., screen time and sleep quality), request evidence,
    identify competing causes, and propose a discriminating test.
  \item \textbf{Source credibility.} Students assess AI-generated claims
    against supplied materials, appraise provenance, and justify whether
    to trust or revise each claim.
  \item \textbf{Evidence-led revision.} Students draft a recommendation,
    interrogate a counterexample, and document how evidence changes their
    position, including residual uncertainty.
\end{itemize}
\noindent Each task is designed for an estimated 15--20~min of interaction
(duration to be calibrated during development). Tasks require age-appropriate
language for 15-year-old learners and carefully curated source materials.
Two AI roles---explanatory assistant (bounded Socratic prompts) and critical
collaborator (counterexample and verification requests)---enable systematic
comparison of interaction conditions.

\noindent\textbf{Observational codes (draft).} Six codes target reasoning
behaviours observable in student turns. Each is recorded per turn with
\texttt{learner\_origin\ =\ indeterminate} (the system does not identify
the cognitive author):\par
\smallskip
\noindent\small
\textbf{EVIDENCE\_REQUEST} --- student requests grounds for a claim\hfill
\textbf{COMPETING\_CAUSE} --- proposes rival explanation\\
\textbf{DISCRIMINATING\_TEST} --- designs comparison to distinguish hypotheses\hfill
\textbf{SOURCE\_APPRAISAL} --- evaluates credibility or relevance\\
\textbf{JUSTIFIED\_REVISION} --- revises conclusion with evidence link\hfill
\textbf{CALIBRATED\_UNCERTAINTY} --- proportional confidence given evidence
\par\normalsize\smallskip
\noindent Blinded human rating with predeclared rubrics and inter-rater
reliability assessment is required before any score is interpreted.
A deterministic heuristic annotation pipeline exists (six keyword codes,
adversarial testing against naive surface scoring) but produces no
proficiency estimate and cannot distinguish learner-originated from
AI-prompted behaviour. The pipeline is a falsification instrument---it
exposes how easily surface codes can be triggered, not a validated
scoring system.

\noindent\textbf{Candidate models and estimated usage.} No model is selected
or deployed. The primary candidate is a current Anthropic model (Claude Opus~4,
API access with contractual prohibition on training from submissions).
Alternative candidates include GPT-4o (OpenAI, hosted API with opt-out), a
self-hosted DeepSeek-V3 (full data control, MIT licence), and Llama~4~Scout
(Meta, community licence, self-hosted). All require adversarial
prompt-leakage testing before assessment use.

Estimated inference per session: approximately 40--150 task-specific
student--AI exchanges, each requiring 1--4 API calls (prompt, critic,
retrieval as needed). At current hosted-API pricing, this yields roughly
\$0.05--\$0.20 per session for the primary model, substantially less for
self-hosted alternatives (compute cost only). A precise cost model
requires specifying the prompt structure, maximum tokens per turn, retry
policy, and infrastructure. These figures are indicative; exact cost
feasibility will be assessed during the development phase.

\section{Intellectual property}

\noindent\textbf{Background.} Existing task concepts, source code, and
documentation developed independently before this collaboration. The current
repository is private with no explicit licence; a permissive open-source
licence (e.g., MIT or Apache~2.0) would be applied before any joint work,
subject to agreement with the OECD on scope and terms.

\noindent\textbf{Foreground.} Co-developed specifications, rubrics, and
protocol designs. A bounded research and evaluation licence to the OECD
would be negotiated, with scope, reuse rights, publication conditions, and
confidentiality agreed in writing before work begins. No current OECD
endorsement or partnership is implied. All third-party model, dataset, and
library rights will be inventoried in the submission.

\section{Privacy, security, and feasibility}

\noindent\textbf{Current state.} The HALA prototype is synthetic only. It
runs on localhost or a private VPS with no external network exposure. It
generates no real participant data and stores no personal information. The
architecture separates assessment logic (gateway on port~8766) from item
storage and scoring (worker on Unix socket) using HMAC-authenticated IPC.
This design has been audited by an independent agent (four audits, 66~tests
passing) but has not undergone formal security certification or penetration
testing, and does not implement OS-level user isolation (all processes run
under a single account).

\noindent\textbf{Future student-facing deployment.} Any pilot must satisfy:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, nosep]
  \item Formal Data Protection Impact Assessment before collection;
  \item Pseudonymisation at collection, restricted retention, controlled
    access, and incident procedures;
  \item Appropriate legal basis under GDPR/DPA (this will not be a simple
    parental consent under Art.~8 alone---roles of data controller and
    processor, risk assessment, and actual pilot conditions must be
    established with the partner institution);
  \item \textbf{No collected student data used for model-provider training},
    verified through contractual terms and, for self-hosted models,
    architectural enforcement (data never leave the deployment host).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Feasibility.} The OECD call does not remunerate the selected
partner. Pyragogy proposes a bounded co-design commitment first (estimated
4--6~months for the deliverables above, requiring approximately 0.5~FTE
research engineer and 0.25~FTE assessment designer). Broader deployment
and validation are conditional on external financing, partner agreements,
and any necessary ethics approvals. All per-session cost estimates are
indicative and derived from current API published pricing---actual costs
depend on final model selection, prompt design, throughput, and
infrastructure decisions made during the collaboration.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Readiness statement.} This proposal describes a planned
development programme, not an existing operational assessment system.
No validated scores, student data, ethics approval, institutional
partnership, or deployed AI tutor exist. The measurement difficulty that
makes the proposal interesting---that AI-mediated interaction can produce
observable evidence of competence, but that evidence does not automatically
constitute a valid measure of student competence---is precisely the object
of study, not a gap to be concealed.
