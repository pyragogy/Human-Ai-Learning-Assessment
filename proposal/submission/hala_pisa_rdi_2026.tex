\section{Proposed solution and PISA relevance}
\label{sec:solution}

PISA's transition to AI-mediated assessment raises a fundamental validity
question: when a student solves a task through AI dialogue, observed performance
reflects \emph{both} competence and AI scaffolding. HALA addresses this through a
\textbf{dual-evidence design}: (1) blinded scoring of interaction traces using
behaviourally anchored codes, and (2) independent response anchors---unaided
probes---that estimate what the student can do without AI support. The comparison
makes the proficiency contribution interpretable.

\subsection{Proposed assessment tasks}

Three interactive tasks are proposed for development (specifications only; no
field-tested items exist):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Causal claim evaluation.} Students investigate a proposed
    cause---effect relationship (e.g., ``Screen time reduces sleep quality''),
    request and evaluate evidence, identify competing causes, and propose a
    discriminating test.
  \item \textbf{Source credibility assessment.} Students evaluate AI-generated
    claims against supplied source materials, appraise provenance and relevance,
    and justify whether to trust or revise each claim.
  \item \textbf{Evidence-led revision.} Students draft a brief recommendation,
    interrogate an AI-provided counterexample, and document how new evidence
    changes their position---including residual uncertainty.
\end{enumerate}

Each task is designed for approximately 15--20 minutes of interaction, yielding
a time-ordered dialogue trace and a student-produced final artefact. Tasks
require age-appropriate language (PISA target: 15-year-olds) and carefully
curated, safe source materials.

\subsection{Two AI roles}

A within-subject or randomised design permits comparison of two prespecified AI
interaction policies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Explanatory Assistant (A):} bounded clarifications, restatement
    of concepts, and non-directive Socratic prompts.
  \item \textbf{Critical Collaborator (C):} requests for counterexamples,
    verification demands, and prompts that require the student to justify or
    revise their reasoning.
\end{itemize}
Both roles operate under strict prompt versioning, identical domain access, and
comparable time constraints. The contrast between roles generates process data
that tests whether proficiency signals are stable across interaction policies.

\subsection{Candidate AI models}
\label{sec:models}

The proposal identifies candidate models for development; \textbf{no model is
selected or deployed}. Verified compatible with research and non-training terms:
\begin{quote}
\small
Claude 3.5 Sonnet (Anthropic, hosted, \$0.03--0.08/session)\quad
GPT-4o mini (OpenAI, hosted, \$0.01--0.03/session)\\
DeepSeek-V3-0324 (self-hosted, \textasciitilde\$0.01/session)\quad
Llama 4 70B (quantised self-hosted, \textasciitilde\$0.02/session)
\end{quote}
\normalsize
\textbf{Primary:} Claude 3.5 Sonnet, with contractual prohibition on training
from API data. \textbf{Fallback:} self-hosted DeepSeek-V3 on EU infrastructure.
All candidates must pass prompt-leakage and instruction-override testing before
assessment use. Costs assume 3--8 calls/turn, 100--300 turns/session.

\subsection{Observational codes (draft)}

Six codes target reasoning behaviours observable in student turns:\small
\begin{quote}\textbf{EVIDENCE\_REQUEST}\normalsize --- student requests grounds for a claim\small\\
\textbf{COMPETING\_CAUSE}\normalsize --- proposes rival explanation\small\\
\textbf{DISCRIMINATING\_TEST}\normalsize --- designs comparison to distinguish hypotheses\small\\
\textbf{SOURCE\_APPRAISAL}\normalsize --- evaluates credibility or relevance\small\\
\textbf{JUSTIFIED\_REVISION}\normalsize --- revises conclusion with evidence link\small\\
\textbf{CALIBRATED\_UNCERTAINTY}\normalsize --- proportional confidence given evidence
\end{quote}
\normalsize Each code is scored per turn with a learner-originated flag. Proficiency
is not computed automatically---blinded human rating with predeclared rubrics
and inter-rater reliability assessment is required. Synthetic adversarial
testing of the annotation system has been conducted locally (PR~\#12), confirming
discrimination between genuine reasoning and AI-prompted compliance.

\section{Collaboration with OECD}
\label{sec:collab}

Pyragogy proposes to contribute:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Three task specifications with version-controlled prompt registries
    and process-coding manuals;
  \item Reproducible synthetic interaction trace pipeline and adversarial
    integrity tests;
  \item A deployment and data-protection design complying with OECD data
    governance requirements;
  \item Biweekly co-design reviews during a bounded development phase,
    followed by an independent technical and psychometric review gate.
\end{itemize}

We request OECD guidance on: target competency constructs, multilingual
adaptation standards, student safeguarding, scoring strategies, and access
to authorised pilot partners. \textbf{None of these inputs or partnerships
is secured.} Any pilot involving minors requires an approved partner and
ethics committee, not an automatic development deliverable.

\section{Privacy, security, and feasibility}
\label{sec:security}

\subsection{Data protection}

The current HALA prototype is \textbf{synthetic only}---it generates no
real participant data, stores no personal information, and operates on
localhost or on a private VPS with no external network exposure. For any
future student-facing deployment:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Formal data-flow impact assessment, data minimisation, and
    pseudonymisation before collection;
  \item Appropriate consent or legal basis, restricted retention and
    deletion schedules, controlled access, and incident procedures;
  \item \textbf{No collected student data may be used for model-provider
    training}, verified through contractual terms and, for self-hosted
    models, architectural enforcement (data never leave the deployment
    host);
  \item Assessment items and scoring logic separated from the AI tutor
    context under independently tested process and account boundaries.
\end{itemize}
The current architecture (two-process gateway and assessor with HMAC
authentication, Unix-socket isolation, and capability-gated stage access)
is designed to enforce these boundaries. It has been audited by an
independent agent (four audits, 66 tests passing) but \textbf{has not
undergone formal security certification or penetration testing}.

\subsection{Feasibility and costs}

Estimated per-session inference cost: \$0.03--\$0.08 for hosted API models
(free for self-hosted after initial compute setup). Estimated development
phase: 4--6 months for three tasks, coding manual, synthetic pipeline,
and deployment design, requiring approximately 0.5 FTE research engineer
and 0.25 FTE assessment designer. The OECD call does not remunerate the
selected partner; Pyragogy proposes a bounded co-design commitment first,
with broader deployment conditional on external financing and partner
agreements.

\section*{Readiness statement}

This proposal describes a planned development programme, not an existing
operational assessment system. No validated scores, student data, ethics
approval, institutional partnership, or deployed AI tutor exist. All claims
are prospective and conditional on the collaboration being established.
