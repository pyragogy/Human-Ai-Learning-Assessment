# Concept note — candidate v0.1 (not yet submitted)

## Human–AI Learning Assessment: Distinguishing Collaborative Performance from Durable Learning

**Proposer:** Pyragogy research initiative; lead researcher: Fabrizio Terzi. Organisational/legal status and partner institutions must be stated accurately in final submission.

### Need
AI-mediated educational assessment faces a construct attribution problem: high-quality student–AI output may reflect AI contributions without corresponding student learning. An assessment that scores only assisted output risks measuring joint task performance rather than retained student competence.

### Objective
Design a process-aware assessment research demonstrator that examines whether critical human–AI collaboration yields measurable, independently demonstrated learning gains, and whether transparent interaction traces contribute valid incremental evidence about learning quality.

### Method
Compare (A) matched non-AI study material, (B) explanatory AI tutoring and (C) critical dialogic AI collaboration. Collect baseline (T0), supervised practice (T1), independent post-test (T2), transfer (T3) and delayed retention (T4). Assisted T1 results are **never** treated as the primary learning outcome. Test forms require calibration, blinded human ratings and sensitivity analysis prior to interpretation.

### Measurement and validation
Propose constructs of conceptual understanding, evidence evaluation, justified reasoning, transfer and retention; exploratory interaction codes include learner hypothesis, alternative explanation, evidence verification and justified revision. Study rating reliability, task equivalence, construct validity, differential functioning, intervention fidelity, effect heterogeneity and item leakage. An Epistemic Quality Index may be investigated as a candidate process construct, not advertised as a validated scale.

### Relevance to PISA
This demonstrator addresses opportunities for interactive AI-mediated assessment, especially evidence evaluation, self-regulation and responsible collaboration. Initial adult feasibility work does **not** establish applicability to international 15-year-old populations. Age adaptation, cross-lingual measurement invariance, international comparability, student safeguarding, and secure platform integration would be separate workstreams subject to partner expertise and approvals.

### Proposed phases and outputs
1. Construct map, prototype item blueprints, controlled AI interaction protocol, fidelity checks.
2. Synthetic-only instrumented demonstrator and tests: reproducible data lineage and independent-outcome separation.
3. Ethics-governed feasibility study (if collaborators and approvals available).
4. Reliability/validity research plan for scaled assessment subject to resources and external review.

### Honest readiness
Prototype data validation and automated tests exist; no learner study has been conducted; no instrument is psychometrically validated; no adolescent study approved; specialist collaborators not yet verified. Scope of unpaid partnership, deliverables, legal status and intellectual property require mutually agreed written terms.

### Decision requested
Consider the approach for a bounded exploratory technical/scientific collaboration in AI-mediated assessment, subject to official call eligibility and resource requirements.
